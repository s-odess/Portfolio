02

Model-in-the-Loop: Tapping a Source Hiding in Plain Sight

A structured government emergency feed had been available for years but sat unused. Previous attempts to extract its drill-down layer automatically had failed. I discovered that layer contained the most useful signal in the system: real-time dispatch data for serious incidents, written in standardized first-responder jargon.

The jargon was the obstacle. I introduced ChatGPT Enterprise with a custom editorial rulebook to translate it into clear user-facing narratives. The rulebook included specific constraints: never substitute 'car' for 'vehicle' (many incidents involved trucks and motorcycles), always attribute information to reports rather than stating it as fact, and flag conflicting accounts explicitly.

The radio pipeline that had anchored the original workflow was degrading from human transcription errors, encryption changes, and model intake failures. The structured text feed was more consistent. User feedback was consistently positive, as the data provided unique real-time perspectives. This workflow became the backbone of coverage in the platform's largest market. Beyond my immediate team (where three other analysts adopted it independently), the manager of the video moderation team and multiple video moderation team members adopted the workflow. It provided vital, real-time contextual data they critically needed to corroborate and validate incoming video feeds, which were frequently vague or unclear regarding active major emergencies in California.

2+ yrs production runtime
~tens of millions additional notifications (est.)
03

Model Output Evaluation: Independent QA at Production Scale

When language models entered the publishing workflow, the existing QA rubric, built for human analysts, missed model-specific failure modes. I built an independent audit system using OpenAI Codex, macOS Shortcuts, and Bash scripts to capture and categorize live outputs, working within normal SLA obligations and without dedicated infrastructure.

1,000+ outputs evaluated
12% peak error rate found

Error categories included factual inaccuracy, policy violations, privacy failures, and formatting bugs. The data-driven insights from these audits were presented directly to executive leadership, driving an objective recalibration of overall publishing and text validation frameworks.

Alongside this audit framework, over five years I submitted 50+ bug and feature tickets across Asana and Jira, translating daily operational observations into structured engineering requirements. Many of these feature proposals subsequently went into production.

Concurrently, after 2022, automated content generation multiplied duplicate push notification series exponentially across geofenced sets. I ran a sustained deduplication operation alongside daily SLA work, manually resolving 100+ broken notification paths to maintain production data integrity.

07

Perspective

Production systems optimize for output. They obscure what isn't working, especially at the edges of model and system reliability.

Independent evaluation from outside the primary workflow surfaces those gaps. Independent evaluation also builds institutional trust, letting teams move faster, because the measurement infrastructure catches failures early, before they compound.

The need for structured, honest measurement grows as workflows scale. It does not disappear; it shifts from catching manual entry errors to evaluating system logic and reasoning quality.
