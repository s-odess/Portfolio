Systems I built

Model-in-the-loop production workflow

Solo build at Citizen on ChatGPT Enterprise

Users submitted video of emergencies that arrived without context. For California, Citizen’s largest market, I designed, built, and ran a model-in-the-loop workflow on ChatGPT Enterprise that translated first-responder jargon into clear narrative around that video, with no engineering or product support. The custom editorial rulebook I wrote for it required source attribution, flagging of conflicting reports, and a human review checkpoint before publishing.

The workflow ran in production for more than two years, cut manual processing by 10 minutes per push series, and drove an estimated 1 million+ additional weekly notifications (my own estimate).

Current state. One California emergency generated duplicate push series that carried little context or narrative.

Future state. One consolidated narrative push series per incident.

Implementation. I built the design as a skill in ChatGPT Enterprise with my manager’s approval, introduced operations teammates to it, trained the head of the video moderation team, and shared training materials through Slack. Analysts on the operations team, the video moderation team, and that team’s manager adopted the sourcing method.

Measurement. I measured the change after launch from user volume and escalation rates.

Output quality evaluation framework

Built at Citizen with OpenAI Codex, macOS Shortcuts, and Bash

I audited more than 1,000 live outputs from Citizen’s automated radio-incident authoring model, a separate system from the workflow above. For the audit I built an independent evaluation framework, checked each output against service-level standards for accuracy, privacy, and editorial quality, defined concrete failure categories, tracked quality in a time-series graph, and surfaced a 12% peak error rate. QA scores rose after a leadership-backed recalibration.

Current state. The authoring model drafted notification copy from first-responder radio clips, and the operations team reviewed each draft for accuracy against the clip and for adherence to Citizen’s editorial, style, privacy, and safety guidelines. Citizen closely tracked the team’s error rate and kept no separate error rate for the model.

Future state. The team, its manager, and leadership know the model’s own error rate.

Implementation. I captured the outputs as screenshots in real time with hotkeys I built in macOS Shortcuts, alongside my regular output and service-level obligations, and audited each one against the standard.

Measurement. Bash scripts generated a time-series graph of quality in half-hour increments, which surfaced the peak.

Arizona regional pipeline

Built at Citizen with an engineer, using Python, JSON, Zapier, Slack, and Tableau

I partnered with an engineer to build a Python, JSON, and Zapier automation that converted jargon-heavy government source text into structured JSON. Together with the step-by-step workflow and quality playbooks I wrote, it scaled Arizona regional output 3x and retention 4x.

Current state. Static and interference on Phoenix and Tucson radio channels caused the team to miss major emergencies.

Future state. Automated notifications drawn from a public real-time feed of fire and medical emergency calls, a design I worked out with the engineer.

Implementation. The automation connected to a nomenclature dictionary that translated first-responder jargon into notification copy, and it ran through Zapier and Slack. I wrote one guide for the operations team and a technical guide for engineers. The engineer and I co-built two Tableau dashboards over Salesforce Service Cloud data to monitor the automated pushes.

Measurement. I measured the result in regional output and retention.

CHP Dispatch Intelligence Pipeline

Independent project built with Python, Playwright, the Gemini API, and the GitHub REST API

I built this Python pipeline to automate a workflow I ran by hand for two years at Citizen. Playwright extracts live California Highway Patrol incident data from a legacy ASP.NET dispatch dashboard and opens each incident’s detail page for the location and coordinates. The pipeline sends the records to the Gemini API, where a constrained prompt turns dispatch shorthand into plain-English summaries. The code retries on rate-limit and server errors, caps each run to protect the API quota, and publishes structured JSON to a public GitHub repository through the GitHub REST API.

How I build. I work through language-model-assisted development with Cursor, OpenAI Codex, Claude Code, Claude, and Gemini: I specify the task, review and test the model’s work, and iterate.

What I learned. The back end came together quickly, while the map front end I tried to build on top of it never worked, so I kept the pipeline and its JSON output. An earlier version captured the full dispatch log from each incident’s detail page, and restoring that depth is my next step.

The pipeline runs from the command line, and the repository includes a GitHub Actions workflow. Read the source code and README on GitHub.

Daily operations at Citizen, 2020–2026

Send decisions and on-call. I owned audience and send decisions for public safety notifications, with increasing responsibility as Citizen scaled. My copy regularly reached more than 1 million users per send and, for some notifications, all 9 million users. I processed radio and other audio source material at 2x speed or faster while holding editorial accuracy standards, and as on-call responder through PagerDuty, I paged engineering and escalated to management during severe incidents. I also wrote bug reports and feature-flag requests in Jira.

Missing persons. I tested hundreds of heading and call-to-action variants for missing-person notifications for clarity and actionability, and I advocated for approximating names from radio coverage, which became policy. Per user and on-the-ground team feedback, notifications using this tested copy assisted in locating four missing people: an 85-year-old woman with dementia in Los Angeles, a missing child in Brooklyn, a missing son in the Bronx, and a teenager located at a New York City train station. I also sourced missing-person cases directly from first-responder social media accounts.

Playbooks. I authored seven step-by-step workflow and quality playbooks that became standard practice across a 100-plus person organization.

Training and quality review. Over roughly five years, I trained and quality-reviewed a rotating subset of about 100 offshore contractors in Kenya and Nepal on US address disambiguation, geography, and police procedure. I coordinated performance issues with Citizen managers and mentored US-based analysts.

Requirements and deduplication. I submitted more than 50 engineering tickets in Jira and Asana that turned daily operational observations into structured requirements, and I coordinated with engineering on data integrity and privacy guardrails. For years I documented duplicate incident notifications in those tickets as automated, offshore, and user-submitted inputs multiplied. Engineering later built a deduplication tool for incidents with video.

Measurement and user research. I analyzed click-through rate, share and disable ratio, estimated net audience growth, and push volume for selected pushes in Tableau, Sigma, and Sundial. Over several months I also conducted user research interviews with the UX research team and reviewed Tableau data and user comments to inform language-model deployment around user needs during Citizen’s expansion into B2B.

Original content sourcing. I partnered with product and UX teams to identify unmet content needs, sourced content from state attorneys general in Illinois and New York, and matched it to critical-incident video coverage, which drove millions of additional context-rich notifications.
