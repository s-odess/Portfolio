02

Model-in-the-Loop: Tapping a Source Hiding in Plain Sight

A structured government emergency feed had been available for years but sat unused. Previous attempts to extract its drill-down layer automatically had failed. I discovered that layer contained the most useful signal in the system: real-time dispatch data for serious incidents, written in standardized first-responder jargon.

The jargon was the obstacle. I introduced ChatGPT Enterprise with a custom editorial rulebook to translate it into clear user-facing narratives. The rulebook included specific constraints: never substitute "car" for "vehicle" (many incidents involved trucks and motorcycles), always attribute information to reports rather than stating it as fact, and flag conflicting accounts explicitly.

The radio pipeline that had anchored the original workflow was degrading from human transcription errors, encryption changes, and model intake failures. The structured text feed was more consistent. Initial objections, including data security concerns, were documented in the proposal before the experiment began; none proved founded, and no further objections arose from leadership or users over two years of production. It became the backbone of coverage in the platform's largest market, and at least three other team members adopted it independently.

2+ yrs production runtime
~tens of millions additional notifications (est.)
03

Model Output Evaluation: Independent QA at Production Scale

When language models entered the publishing workflow, the existing QA rubric, built for human analysts , missed model-specific failure modes. I built an independent audit system using OpenAI Codex, macOS Shortcuts, and Bash scripts to capture and categorize live outputs, working within normal SLA obligations and without dedicated infrastructure.

1,000+ outputs evaluated
12% peak error rate found

Error categories: factual inaccuracy, policy violations, privacy failures, formatting bugs. Findings presented to leadership drove QA recalibration across teams.

Separate from the audit: over five years I submitted 50+ bug and feature tickets across Asana and Jira, translating operational observations into structured engineering requests. Many feature proposals went into production.

Also separate: after 2022, LLMs multiplied duplicate push notification series exponentially across geofenced sets. I ran a sustained deduplication operation alongside daily SLA work, manually resolving 100+ broken notification paths to maintain production integrity.

07

Perspective

Production systems optimize for output. They obscure what isn't working, especially at the edges of model reliability.

Independent evaluation from outside the primary workflow surfaces those gaps. Independent evaluation also builds institutional trust, letting teams move faster , because the measurement infrastructure catches failures early, before they compound.

The need for structured, honest measurement grows as models do. It does not disappear; it shifts from catching factual errors to evaluating reasoning quality.
