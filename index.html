Model-in-the-Loop Workflow: Tapping a Source Hiding in Plain Sight

A live online government emergency data source was available to our team for years but was underutilized. Previous attempts to automate extraction from its drill-down feature, which contains the granular incident detail users need, failed repeatedly. I initially discovered that drill-down feature within our team and recognized what it contained: standardized first-responder jargon describing serious emergencies in real time, with a level of specificity that no other source in our workflow provided.

The jargon was the obstacle. Terse, highly abbreviated, and written for dispatch operators rather than general audiences, it was not usable in a user-facing notification workflow without translation. I introduced ChatGPT Enterprise with a customized editorial rulebook as the translation layer, building custom prompt guardrails to protect output integrity and converting raw incident data into clear, context-rich narratives. For example, I directed the LLM to never use “car” as a synonym for “vehicle,” as many emergencies involved trucks and motorcycles. I gave it specific logic to minimize context collapse errors by attributing information to reports and mentioning conflicting reports.

The timing was critical. The first-responder radio pipeline, the original foundation of Citizen's workflow, was at risk. Human transcription errors, market changes, model intake failures, and increasing radio encryption were all reducing its reliability across key markets. The government URL workflow we built was a cleaner substitute. The signal-to-noise ratio exceeded radio: the source delivered structured text rather than audio, the jargon followed more standardized conventions than spoken dispatch, and the models grew increasingly capable at the translation task as they improved.

The use case centered on Citizen's largest market, where major vehicle collisions on freeways generated serious emergencies that users had no reliable way to track in real time. Incidents with corroborated text narratives regularly reached polygon notification distributions in the hundreds of thousands of users, often activating previously inactive accounts. Applied across hundreds of collision and fire emergencies per week, the integration made possible an estimated tens of millions of additional notifications over its two-plus year run.

The proposal faced initial organizational resistance. I documented the objections formally before proceeding, addressed data security and editorial concerns in writing, and received approval to run the experiment. It ran continuously for over two years, became the backbone of the workflow for that market, and at least three additional team members, including the video team manager, adopted it. In this way, we placed a model in the loop and maintained its effectiveness.

Independent LLM QA Auditing, Ticket Triage & Queue Management

When automated language models entered high-stakes publishing workflows, existing evaluation rubrics designed for human analysts failed to capture critical model edge cases and hallucinations.

To resolve these hidden product errors, I independently built a lightweight quality assurance infrastructure. Using OpenAI Codex, macOS Shortcuts and Bash scripts, I designed an adversarial auditing framework that captured and cataloged over 1,000 live model outputs across factual inaccuracy, compliance gaps, and formatting bugs while maintaining daily turnaround SLAs. The audit exposed an implicit 12% peak error rate, which I presented to organizational leadership to immediately recalibrate cross-functional QA standards and update frontline guidelines.

In parallel, I managed continuous error-triage queues, converting automated system discrepancies into clear engineering tickets to bridge the communication gap between support operations and backend developers. To mitigate exponential model duplication errors after 2022, I spearheaded a merge workflow for geofenced notification sets, manually resolving and deduplicating over 100 broken notification paths to maintain production integrity under pressure.

Perspective

Scaling language AI systems and honestly evaluating them require different orientations. Production environments optimize to surface what works, which means they tend to obscure what does not, particularly at the boundaries of model reliability.

Human oversight from outside the primary optimization loop surfaces these gaps. It also builds the institutional trust that lets organizations move faster on AI adoption, because the measurement infrastructure catches failures early rather than at scale.

This function does not disappear as models improve. It evolves in form and focus, shifting from catching factual errors to evaluating reasoning quality, from single-model audits to cross-model governance. The need for structured, honest measurement is durable.
